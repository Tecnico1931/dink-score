1. What the app is and who it is for

Dink Score is a pickleball scorekeeper and club record in one, built for a single friend group rather than a league or a marketplace. Someone holding a phone keeps score on court (it tracks the score call, who is serving, and which side to serve from). Afterwards the group looks at results, ratings and standings together.

The off-court half is the point: every game feeds a Dink Rating (the app's own 2.00 to 8.00 skill rating), a leaderboard, achievements and weekly challenges, so the rivalry keeps people coming back. Around that sits a set of club tools for organizers: game nights, tournaments with brackets, leagues and ladders, an open-play court board, and a TV page.

It is a website that installs to the phone like an app (no app store), works without signal at the courts, and needs no account: a club is a 6-character code that everyone's phone shares. There is also an optional passkey account, an optional Apple Watch app, and an optional DUPR link.

The header

  • App mark (top left).
  • Club chip ("Join a club" when you have none, or the club's name): opens the club sheet, which holds the club code, the invite link and QR, the TV page, live games, game nights, linked clubs, the organizer PIN and all organizer tools.

3. Each screen in turn

Who may do it uses these words (explained in section 5): anyone (even with no club), club member (anyone holding the club code), linked player (a member whose phone has claimed a player name), organizer (the creating phone, a co-organizer, or a phone unlocked with the organizer PIN).

Play (home)

Shows a greeting and a seven-day bar chart of games, the club's "This week" numbers, two big buttons (New game, Log a result), the top doubles player, three weekly challenges, a "Your Dink" card if the club has rewards on, the next game night, the organizer's pinned announcement (if any), a monthly club summary and recent games. A paused game offers Resume.

  • Who: anyone can score; saving to the club needs a club member.

New game (setup)

Pick doubles or singles, side-out or rally scoring, play to 11, 15 or 21, and win by 2 or 1. Type names or tap saved players (with their current rating beside each). Choose who serves first, and see a score forecast from the ratings ("Too close to call, 11 to 10"). A linked player who has saved paddles can pick one.

The live scoring screen (the court)

Two big halves, blue and green. Tap the half of the side that won the rally. The app applies the side-out rules itself and shows the three-number score call (serving score, receiving score, server 1 or 2), who is serving and from which side, and prompts for change of ends. Controls along the top: Leave (pause and come back), Sunlight display, Share this game (code and QR), Log a result from another court, Undo. Link a watch to this game sits under the call.

  • Who: a club member scores. Any phone or watch holding the game's secret can also score the same game (see "live shared games" in section 5).

Game over

When a side wins, a sheet shows the score, rallies and duration, the forecast, and the rating change for all four players before saving. Save and rematch or Save and finish. Share makes an image. Undo last rally is still there.

Log a result

A form for a finished game you didn't score live (format, target, scores, players, finish time). It counts exactly like a live game. Who: a club member (or anyone, kept on the phone, with no club).

Leaders

A ranked table per format (Doubles or Singles) for a season or All time, then Club honors (most wins, longest win streak, best point difference, most games). Each row shows record, points, reliability, rating and last change. Players with fewer than 10 games show Provisional. Tap a name for their profile, or the little change pill for "why my rating moved".

Record

Counts (games, rallies, minutes) and every finished game with Replay (live-scored games only), Share, Edit and Delete, plus + Log a result. Deleted games stay restorable for 30 days.

  • Who: anyone in the club can read. Edit, Delete and Restore need the organizer PIN or the creating phone (api/games.js calls requirePin).

Rules

Plain-English scoring and serving rules, the kitchen, how Dink Rating works, achievements/challenges/seasons, DUPR set-up, and privacy, terms and security.

Events tab

Three blocks: Game nights, Events (with All events and New event), and Leagues and ladders. When something is live, a "Standings now" card appears on top.

  • Who: anyone in the club can answer a game night and watch; creating events, leagues and ladders is organizer-only; entering results is limited to the players in a match and organizers unless an organizer switches on "Let any club member enter results".

4. The features, in plain words

Each entry says what it does, where to find it and who may use it.

Scoring a live game

  • What: you set up a game, then tap the half of the screen for whoever won each rally. The app handles the side-out rules: only the serving side scores, server 1 then server 2, then side out; the score call (for example 4-2-1); the serve side (even score from the right, odd from the left); change of ends.
  • Options: doubles or singles; side-out or rally scoring; to 11, 15 or 21; win by 2 or 1.
  • Safety nets: Undo; Leave pauses the game so you can come back (Play then shows Resume); the game is kept on the phone and sent to the club when it ends, queued if there is no signal (see the outbox in section 5).
  • Where: Play, then New game. Who: anyone; a club member's game is saved to the club.

Logging a result

  • What: add a finished game from another court (even while your own game is running). It counts toward the record and ratings exactly like a live game.
  • Where: Play or Record, then + Log a result; also the + on the court. Who: anyone on their own phone; club members to the club record.

Dink Rating and Leaders

  • What: the app's own skill rating, from 2.00 to 8.00. Everyone starts at 2.00 and it never goes below it (the engine's floor is 2.00, seen in the demo: a player with 2 wins and 5 losses sits at 2.00). Doubles and singles are rated separately. A team's rating is the average of its players.
  • How it moves: before a game the app forecasts the score from the ratings. After it, your rating moves by how far you beat or missed that forecast, so losing 9-11 to a much stronger team can raise it. The share of points used is capped at 11-3 (about 79%), so running up the score does not help. Reliability climbs to 100% at 20 games and fades after a month away; until your 10th game you are marked Provisional and you move faster. Logged results count the same as live ones. (Constants: rating-engine.js line 17: START 2, MIN 2, MAX 8, FULL 20, PROVISIONAL 10, CAP 11/14.)
  • Where: Leaders (table, seasons, Club honors); setup shows the forecast; the win sheet shows each player's change; tap a change pill for "why my rating moved".
  • Who: everyone in the club sees it. One engine is shared by the app and the server so they always agree.

Game nights (In / Maybe / Out)

  • What: an organizer schedules a night (title, date, start time, place, optional player limit, optional note). Everyone answers In, Maybe or Out; the card shows spots taken ("1/12 spots") and who is in. Once full, others can still say Maybe or Out. Organizers can Remind, Edit or Cancel night.
  • Where: Events tab; the next night also appears on Play and in the bell inbox.
  • Who: organizers create; members answer.

Events and tournaments, brackets, standings and the TV view

  • Formats: round robin, pool play then knockout, single elimination, double elimination. Partners rotate every round or are fixed teams. Rotation can be fair rotation, random draw or I pick; extra players sit out in turn. Up to 8 courts play at once; 1 to 4 divisions.
  • Scoring: each match is scored live (a normal court screen) or by typing the final score; Fix score corrects one. Each finished match is saved once to the club's record, with a game id made from the event and match (<eventId>-<matchId>) so ratings never count it twice.
  • Standings: wins, then point difference.
  • Brackets page: draws the knockout as a bracket (byes, where winners and losers go next, the champion). Round robins explain they have no bracket.
  • Public link and TV view: organizers can Share bracket: an unguessable link /b/<token> that shows the bracket, standings and champion with no way into the club, refreshing by itself. ?tv=1 (or the TV mode button) hides everything but the bracket and fits it to a big screen.
  • Also: import players and teams from a Google Sheet or CSV, optional team names and logos, Export for DUPR (a CSV and copyable text of finished matches).
  • Where: Events tab, New event (organizer), the event page, Brackets.
  • Who: organizers create, plan, re-pair, start the knockout and fix scores; the players in a match (or anyone, if an organizer allows) enter results; everyone in the club can watch; anyone with the public link can watch.

The club TV page

Separate from the bracket TV: open /?display=CODE on a monitor at the courts for a full-screen sign with the club name, a big QR code (the invite link), the club code and, when the court board is on, who is on each court and who is waiting. It is reached from the club sheet ("Show the TV display page").

Leagues and ladders

  • League: a season of weekly matches. Everyone in a division plays everyone (once, or home and away); the table decides the season. Organizers Plan this week (day, court), Tell the players, can Move or mark Forfeit a match, Finish the season now and start the next one (the top of each division moves up). A public league page lives at /l/<token>.
  • Ladder: players (or doubles teams) challenge someone a few places above (default up to 3). Win and you take their place; lose and nothing changes. Decay (new): an idle player can slip down one place per 7, 14 or 30 days without a challenge, or never. Open challenges (new): a challenge is sent, then accepted or declined, and lapses after 7 days (CHALLENGE_DAYS = 7). Close this season keeps the order as the next season's start.
  • Where: Events tab, "Leagues and ladders", then New league or ladder. Who: organizers create; the players in a match (or any member, if allowed) enter results.
  • Not verified in the browser: the challenge send/accept/decline screens and idle decay. I built a ladder (shots below) but, as the top-ranked player on the only phone, I could not send a challenge, and decay needs days to pass. They are described from api/_league.js (unverified in the running app).

Pickup sessions

  • What: for playing with people who are not in your club. A pickup session is a shared space with its own 8-character code and QR. Anyone who joins can add games and see results. It lasts 24 hours, then everything in it is deleted. It is not part of any club unless a member saves its games to one.
  • Where: "Playing with people you don't know? Pickup session" on the New game screen or the Join a club sheet. Who: anyone; only the host (a secret kept on the host's phone) can remove games or end it early.

The court board (open play)

  • What: for open play with a queue. Players check in, an organizer (or any checked-in player, depending on a setting) calls the suggested group to a free court, and when a game ends the board suggests the next group, spread so people meet new partners. Options: courts (1 to 12), doubles or singles, "everyone goes to the back" or "winners stay" up to 9 games, and who runs the courts. It shows on the club TV page. It never saves a game: the scorer's normal save does, so ratings cannot double count.
  • Where: club sheet, Open play court board. An organizer turns it on first. Who: organizers configure; linked players check themselves in.

Replay

A game scored live keeps a rally-by-rally log. Replay (Record tab) shows a score-flow chart, the longest run, side-outs, and a step-through of the whole game. Games typed in with Log a result have no replay. Edits keep the log only if it still replays to the edited score; event score corrections clear it.

DUPR link (and the self-reported rating)

  • Self-reported rating: a player can type an optional DUPR rating on their profile. It is labelled "self-reported, not verified", visible to the club, never sent to DUPR, and never feeds the Dink Rating, achievements, leaderboard order or event seeding.
  • Real DUPR link: a player connects their DUPR account (they sign in on DUPR's own page; Dink Score never sees the password), sees DUPR ratings labelled as DUPR's, and an organizer can send finished event matches to DUPR after a review screen and an explicit confirm. Without DUPR keys on the server this is invisible.
  • Where: You sheet, DUPR (per club); Rules, "Set up DUPR"; organizers: event, review and send. Who: a player or an organizer for a player; sending is organizer-only.
  • Status: the real DUPR link only appears when the server has DUPR keys, and it has only been tested against a local mock (tools/dupr-mock.mjs) (unverified against real DUPR).

Achievements, weekly challenges, seasons and rivalries

  • 12 achievements (one-time badges): First win, Regular (10 games), Court rat (50), Centurion (100), Heater (5 wins in a row), Unstoppable (10), Shutout, Marathon (win past the target), Giant killer (win when the forecast gave you 35% or less), Social butterfly (win with 5 partners), Two point five (reach 2.50), Elite (reach 3.00).
  • Weekly challenges: three goals each week (from a pool of 8, such as "Win 3 games" or "Win with a partner you've never won with"), the same for everyone, shown on Play. Weeks start Monday 00:00 UTC.
  • Seasons: calendar quarters (Winter, Spring, Summer, Fall). Each season's table restarts everyone at 2.00; the top 3 with 5 or more games win a permanent trophy. Organizers can end a season early and name the next.
  • Rivalries, partners and head-to-head: on every profile.
  • Fairness rule: only games sent from a linked player's phone (or the club creator's) count toward these, and weeks and seasons use the server's receive time, so backdating does not help.
  • Where: your profile; Play (challenges); Leaders (season picker).

Clubs and joining with a code

  • What: a club is one shared record (games, saved players, leaderboard) with a 6-character code. Anyone with the code can see and add games. Join by typing the code, opening an invite link, or scanning the QR (the TV page shows one). Start a club with a name and an organizer PIN (6 to 8 digits) or let the app generate an 8-digit organizer code shown once.
  • Rotating the code: an organizer can change the club code; the old code keeps working for a while.
  • Choosing your name: after joining you tell the app which saved player you are (a claim). Your phone then holds a private key for that name, so your games count toward your achievements.
  • Confirmation (optional): organizers can require that games be confirmed by the other team or an organizer before they count ("Count only confirmed games"); the app also flags possible duplicates (the same four players and score recorded twice).
  • Where: club chip; Organizer tools. Who: anyone joins; organizers manage.

Linked clubs

Two friendly clubs can share one leaderboard. Both sides must agree: one organizer makes an invite code (valid 7 days, used once), the other enters it, reads who it is from, and confirms. The shared board is visible only to the members of the linked clubs, never public. Where: club sheet, "Linked clubs". Who: organizers.

Passkey sign-in (optional account)

An optional account with no password: you sign in with a passkey, the fingerprint, face or screen lock you already use. It brings your clubs, player names and organizer access to another phone, and a lost phone no longer loses them. A passkey works only on the web address it was made on; another address gets one through a one-time code. The Home Screen app is a separate copy from Safari, so sign in there too. Where: "Play as a club" or You, then Account and passkey. Who: anyone. I could not test passkey creation: it needs a real device authenticator; the server side is covered by tools/account-check.mjs with a software authenticator.

Moving to a new phone

No account needed: on the old phone choose Move to a new phone (club sheet) and get a transfer code that works once, for 10 minutes; type it on the new phone. The code never reaches the server; the phones use it to encrypt and look up the move, so the server holds only an id it cannot reverse and a blob it cannot read. Lost the old phone? Only the person who created a club has a 16-character recovery code; it restores organizer access, not a player name. A member can also ask an organizer to approve linking their name to a new phone (the request lasts 15 minutes).

The Apple Watch app

A separate watch-only SwiftUI app (no iPhone app) that scores a game from the wrist and records it as a pickleball workout. It talks straight to the server over HTTPS. Linking: the watch shows a square QR code plus 6 characters and 4 numbers; on the phone open You, Apple Watch, Link a watch and scan or type them (or link just one game from the court's "Link a watch to this game"). Taps go into an outbox on the watch and are sent in order, so a flaky connection does not lose points. The scoring phone does not need to stay open: the server holds the score. Reality check: it is built from source with Xcode onto your own watch (a free Apple ID works for 7 days at a time); it is not on the App Store and has not been run on a real watch (only a simulator and tests). There is an older "Score with Shortcuts" route in the same sheet. Who: a member.

Notifications and the inbox

  • Inbox (bell): always on, built on the phone. "Needs you" (a game to confirm, a night to answer, a refused change) disappears when done; "Recent" (a night changed, a round went live, league results) is kept per club on that phone only.
  • Push alerts (optional): per club and per phone, you choose categories: game nights, games to confirm, events and courts, league. They go only to the phone of a club member, and carry a first name and a court number, never the club code or a score. Push stays off until the server has push keys (VAPID_* in the hosting settings), and on iPhone only works from the Home Screen app. Where: You, Notifications.
  • Status: push delivery was not verified end to end (the demo had no push keys).

The rewards economy (Dinks and the daily streak)

  • What exists today: a daily play streak, free Dinks (play money) and a Shop where Dinks buy cosmetic looks. The first game of each new day earns 1 Dink; the streak earns bonuses on day 3 (+2), 7 (+5), 14 (+10) and 30 (+25), and every 30th day after that. A "Your Dink" card on Play shows the days and Dinks; a flame appears on the avatar from a 2-day streak. Dinks cannot be bought or cashed out and never change a game, a score, a rating, a rank or a pairing.
  • Off by default: an organizer turns it on per club (Organizer tools, Rewards); each player can hide it on their own phone (You sheet). Off means no rewards UI, copy or calls.
  • How it is kept: the server holds an append-only ledger; a phone's balance is worked out from games that phone's linked name saved, using the server's clock, so a phone cannot back-date to earn. A first sync pays at most 14 past days.
  • The Shop: a linked player opens Shop from the "Your Dink" card and spends Dinks on cosmetic items at fixed prices that the server sets: a ring around your picture (Court green ring 6, Team blue ring 8, Ink ring 8, Double ring 15) or a colour for your paddle thumbnails (Court green 6, Team blue 8, Ink 8, Striped 15). Each item is bought once and can be worn or taken off. It changes only how your own picture or paddle thumbnail looks, never a game, a score, a rating, a rank or a pairing, and nothing in the Shop is random. Dinks still cannot be bought with real money.

What's new

A one-time "What's new" sheet shows after an update (remembered on the phone). The current one, dated 2026-11-22, lists the bell and inbox, per-category alerts, the single You sheet, Events and game nights, the court look, ladder decay, leagues and ladders, passkey sign-in and the Apple Watch.

Display settings

On the You sheet, Display and sound (saved on this device only): Appearance (match phone, light, dark), Sunlight contrast (white page, black text, solid team colours), Court scores (normal or large), Win celebration (optional confetti and a short chime when a game ends; confetti stays off with reduced motion) and Celebration sound. A court screen has its own sunlight toggle too.

Privacy and data export

  • You can download or erase your own data: You, Your data: Download my data, or Anonymise me (your name is replaced by a random "Former player" label across the club; games stay so other people's ratings stay correct; your picture, DUPR link and phone link are removed; cannot be undone).
  • Organizers can export the whole club: Download club (JSON) and Games (CSV), and Delete club: type the club's name; everything is removed after 24 hours and can be undone until then. Only the creating phone or the PIN can delete a club, not a co-organizer.
  • Pages: /privacy and /terms describe what the app stores and the terms of use; both are marked as drafts.
  • Analytics: Vercel Web Analytics is first-party and cookie-less, sends only page views with masked addresses (no club code, no share token), and sends nothing if the browser says Do Not Track.

Smaller features worth knowing

  • Reactions on finished games: clap "Great game", fire "On fire", pickle "Nice dink", sweat "Close one", trophy "Clutch" (a member can leave up to 3 per game).
  • Paddles: save your paddles (with photo), pick one when you start a game, and see your record with each (private to your phone's linked name).
  • Pictures: a profile picture or initials; a club holds at most 50 photos.
  • Pinned note and how to pay (organizer): an announcement banner on Play and a payment link.
  • Public result links (organizer switch, off by default): members can make a read-only link and a preview card for a finished game or a day's games. Names on those games become visible to whoever has the link.
  • Year in review: a per-player 2026 recap and a shareable image.
  • Game history: organizers can see who recorded, edited, deleted or confirmed each game. Undo reverses a role change or rename within 7 days.
  • Attendance and busiest hours: totals only; no player is listed.
  • My games across clubs: your own games from every club your phone is linked in.
  • Games going on: every live game in the club, scoring it needs that game's secret.

5. How it works under the hood (plain language)

A website that installs like an app

Dink Score is a web app (a PWA). A small manifest and a service worker (sw.js) let a phone install it to the home screen, open it full screen, and keep working without signal. On install the service worker saves the app's pages and fonts (the "shell"); after that the app opens from the phone. It deliberately never caches the live data (/api/), the public bracket and result pages (/b/, /r/), or the privacy and terms pages. Each release bumps the worker's VERSION (now dink-score-v140) so phones pick up the new copy.

One inline page, built from source files

The whole app is one big HTML page (about 8,500 lines, 730 KB). Its source is dink-score.html (just the page body), plus 33 smaller module files in src/app/ (rewards, game nights, pickup sessions, the Apple Watch link, and so on). A build step, node build.cjs, pastes the modules back into the page between marker comments, wraps it with the manifest and head, writes index.html and the public/ folder for hosting, and rewrites vercel.json with a hash of every inline script. The browser only runs scripts whose hash is on that list (a Content Security Policy), which blocks injected code. A few shared blocks (the rating engine, the scoring rules, the bracket drawing, the replay chart) are written once and pasted into several places, with tests that pin the copies to the same numbers. The same page can also be published as a Claude artifact, where the club features are hidden.

Server functions and the database

The server is a set of Vercel Functions in api/ backed by a Neon Postgres database (its connection string lives in the hosting settings). There are 11 public routes: club, games, roster, players, events, bracket, live, account, transfer, dupr and report. Files starting with _ are helpers, not routes. The hosting plan allows 12 functions, so new features are folded into an existing route (leagues ride on events; push, pickup sessions, the court board and watch scoring ride on live; result pages ride on bracket). Tables are created on first use and only ever added to (CREATE ... IF NOT EXISTS), never changed destructively. Main tables: clubs, roster (saved players and their phone links), games, game_audit, events, leagues, game_nights and game_night_rsvps, live_games, court_boards, pickup_*, reward_ledger and reward_items, rating_history, push_subs, accounts and passkeys, watch_*, transfers, dupr_*, result_shares, club_links. Per-club tables cascade from clubs, so deleting a club removes its rows.

Offline outbox and sync

The phone keeps a working copy of the club in its own storage, so screens open instantly and scoring works with no signal. Anything you create (a finished game, an edit, a roster change) goes into an outbox first and is sent when the network allows; a request that hangs for 15 seconds counts as offline and retries. A change the server refuses is never thrown away: it stays in the outbox marked failed until someone retries, corrects the score, or exports it as a file. Polling is cheap: the server answers "nothing changed" (an ETag and an empty 304) before reading any game. Everything read back from storage passes through a repair layer (Heal) first, and anything unusable is set aside rather than deleted, so a damaged or newer-format save shows a recovery screen instead of a blank page.

Live shared games, and the watch

When you score a club game, the server holds the game (api/live.js). Any phone or watch that has the game's secret can send a rally; the server applies it with the same scoring rules as the app (a server copy, api/_pb.js, tested against the app's rules) and a version check keeps simultaneous taps in order. Every screen follows the server's state within a few seconds. The club's "Games going on" list shows live games with scores. The watch links by a one-time QR plus four numbers, then sends taps (each with its own id, so a repeated tap is counted once) to the same route.

What to try first (ten steps)

Use a throwaway club, never a real one, and delete it at the end.

  1. Open https://dinkscore.vercel.app on a phone, watch the tour, then add it to the home screen (Share, Add to Home Screen on iPhone; the browser menu, Install app on Chrome).
  2. Score a game with no club. Play, New game, doubles, type four names, Start. Tap the winning half a few times, watch the call change, try Undo, then Leave and Resume.
  3. Read the Rules tab, especially "How Dink Rating works".
  4. Start a test club. Tap Join a club, then "Start a new club"; give it a name and leave the PIN blank so the app generates an organizer code. Write that code down.
  5. Join from a second phone or browser with the club code (or scan the QR in the club sheet), and pick your name.
  6. Finish and save three or four games (score one live, log the rest). Open Leaders, then tap a rating pill for the "why it moved" screen; open the Record tab and try Replay and Share.
  7. Schedule a game night (Events, Schedule a game night), answer In on the second phone, and look at the bell.
  8. Run a small event. Events, New event, round robin, 8 players, 2 courts. Enter a few scores, open Standings, tap Brackets, then Share bracket and open the link on a laptop with TV view.
  9. Look at the organizer side. Club sheet: Organizer tools, Members, the court board, the TV page, Your data. Switch on Rewards and see the "Your Dink" card.
  10. Clean up. Club sheet, Your data, delete the club (type its name; it is removed after 24 hours and can be undone until then).
